# CityConnect Service Request Data Analysis

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyses the CityConnect resolved service requests dataset using Python.

The purpose is to understand how efficiently CityConnect resolves resident service requests, identify factors associated with longer resolution times, and explore how service delivery affects resident satisfaction.

The analysis uses descriptive statistics, visualisations and introductory data analysis techniques.

## 2. Business Question

How can CityConnect use historical service request data to identify resolution delays, improve operational efficiency and increase resident satisfaction?

The analysis focuses on:
- Average service request resolution times.
- Differences in resolution times across service types and regions.
- Factors associated with longer waiting times.
- Relationships between resolution times and resident satisfaction.
- Identifying service delivery problems that require management attention.

## 3. Dataset

The project uses `cityconnect_resolved_requests.csv`.

The cleaned dataset contains 420 service request records.

Important variables include:
- request_id
- opened_on
- closed_on
- service_type
- region
- priority
- resolution_days
- satisfaction_rating
- travel_minutes
- resident_comment

The original CSV file was not manually modified during the analysis.

- The distribution is positively skewed, meaning a smaller number of requests experience very long delays.

## 5. Additional Analysis Techniques (PA0306)

### Regression Analysis
An introductory regression model was used to explore factors associated with resolution times.

Water service requests had the largest displayed positive coefficient (+6.63), suggesting longer resolution times relative to the reference service category.

The residual standard deviation was 8.26 days, indicating that the model still has considerable prediction error.

### Monte Carlo Simulation
Resampling was used to estimate the probability of a request taking longer than 14 days.

Estimated probability: 16.9%.

This means approximately 17 out of every 100 requests may take longer than 14 days.

### Correlation Analysis
The strongest relationship was between resolution days and satisfaction rating (-0.59).

This suggests that longer resolution times are associated with lower resident satisfaction.

Travel time had a weak positive correlation with resolution days (0.22).

### Cohort Analysis
Monthly comparisons showed that April 2026 had the longest average resolution time at 10.56 days.

The monthly pattern was mixed rather than consistently improving.

### Cluster Analysis
Three clusters were identified:

- Cluster 0: Slow resolution and very low satisfaction.
- Cluster 1: Fast resolution and high satisfaction.
- Cluster 2: Moderate resolution times and satisfaction.

Cluster 0 should receive the highest operational priority.

### Time Series Analysis
Weekly request volumes were relatively steady, with some fluctuations.

Median resolution times did not consistently increase with request volumes.

### Sentiment Analysis
Resident comments were classified into three sentiment categories:

- Negative: 172 comments.
- Positive: 161 comments.
- Neutral: 87 comments.

Negative comments had an average resolution time of 16.18 days, compared with 3.21 days for positive comments.

This highlights the relationship between service delays and resident experience.

## 6. Business Recommendations

Based on the analysis, CityConnect should:

1. Prioritise Water service requests because they have the longest average resolution time.
2. Investigate requests with exceptionally long resolution times.
3. Improve communication with residents experiencing delays.
4. Investigate the operational causes of the slow-resolution, low-satisfaction cluster.
5. Monitor monthly and weekly performance to identify emerging problems.
6. Use satisfaction ratings and sentiment analysis alongside operational performance measures.
